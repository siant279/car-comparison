Charging Specifications — All Four Cars
Key insight: P12 charges more slowly per stop than P10
The P10 and P12 share the same 370 kW peak DC and same NACS port — but the P12's 117 kWh battery (vs 91 kWh usable on P10) takes longer to fill. P12 does 10–80% in ~19 min vs P10's ~16 min. The P10 also averages ~247 kW across the session vs P12's ~215–230 kW. Counterintuitively, the P10 is the faster charger per stop, even though the P12 has more range. For a family with a toddler, more stops with shorter pauses (P10) can actually be preferable to fewer stops with slightly longer waits (P12).

Real-World Range: Weather, Altitude & Conditions
The Truckee-to-Colorado route — a gauntlet of conditions
Your road trip to Steamboat passes through everything: Donner Pass (7,056 ft), Nevada high desert (4,000–6,000 ft, summer 100°F+ or winter 15°F), Salt Lake basin, Wasatch grades up to 8,000 ft, and Colorado mountains at 7,000–9,000 ft. Each condition affects range differently. A strong 10 mph headwind on I-80 through Nevada adds ~19% to energy consumption. Summer A/C in 105°F desert heat costs 5–15% range. Winter cold on the Wasatch can cost 20–30%.

✅ High altitude actually helps EV range
Colorado's high altitude reduces air density significantly — an EV at 8,000 ft experiences ~20% less aerodynamic drag than at sea level. Research shows Utah, Colorado, and New Mexico EVs consistently outperform EPA range estimates in summer months due to thin air. The climb costs energy, but sustained highway cruising at altitude in summer is notably more efficient. This partially offsets the grade penalties on the route.

❄️ February trip verdict
The BMW iX3 and EX60 P12 are the most relaxed February drivers — 3 stops, large batteries, comfortable buffers on every cold segment. The decision tree — with confirmed prices from official PDFs

BMW vs Volvo P10 — price is now a tie.
→ At $67,100 vs $67,265 (before roof racks), both cars cost essentially the same. The choice is now purely about what matters most: BMW = more range + winter buffer + fewer road trip stops. Volvo = better child safety + Google Gemini AI + faster charge stops + available sooner.

Do you finance the purchase and earn under $200K (MFJ)?
→ The R2 at ~$52,710 effective is ~$14–15k cheaper than BMW or Volvo P10 after OBBBA. That's still a large and meaningful gap. R2 becomes the strong default if adventure capability and value dominate.

Is the monthly SF Bay round trip in January without charging a hard requirement?
→ Only the P12 (~$74,265, ~305 mi winter) and iX3 ($67,100, ~325–335 mi winter) can reliably do it. The P10 (~248 mi winter) cannot. Notably the iX3 now costs ~$7k less than the P12 for comparable winter range.

Do you want the car now — not fall/late 2026?
→ R2 (delivering now) or P10 Plus (summer 2026). iX3 doesn't land until Fall 2026; P12 late 2026.

Does the Thule roof box on ski trips shift the P10 vs P12 decision?
→ Yes materially. P10 + loaded box in February = ~203 mi effective range. P12 + box = ~252 mi. That extra 50 miles removes the need for a cautious extra stop in Nevada. If you use the box on every Steamboat trip, the P12 case strengthens.

Is the self-driving highway assist worth $2,500 extra on the BMW?
→ It's not in your current BMW build. If you want hands-free I-80 Nevada driving, add Driving Assistance Pro ($2,500) = $70,120 total — now $2,355 more than the Volvo.

Overall recommendation — EX60 P10 Plus at $67,265 (+ roof bars ~$67,765)
Now that the confirmed prices are in, the BMW and Volvo P10 are at the same price. The P10 wins on family grounds: Google Gemini AI, multi-adaptive safety belt, integrated booster seat for your daughter, fastest charge stops (16 min), air purification, and available summer 2026. The BMW wins on range and winter buffer — if those matter more to you, it's now a legitimate equal-cost alternative rather than the more expensive option it seemed to be.

Specific scenario upgrades: If you do SF in January monthly without stopping → seriously consider iX3 (same price, much better winter range) or P12 (~$6,500–7k more).
